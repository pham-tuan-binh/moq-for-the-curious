# MOQT, delivery, and security audit — 2026-09-22

Scope: chapters 04, 05, 06, and 08, including their Mermaid sequences, tables, numerical examples, and citations. Reviewed the full chapter sources and the relevant sections of the actual pinned draft, not search-result summaries. This is a source audit, not an interoperability or security test of an implementation.

## Corrections

- Chapter 04 now explains duplicate delivery when fill and live subscription ranges overlap.
- Chapter 05 distinguishes fixed payloads from object properties that permit updates.
- Chapter 06 distinguishes recommended scheduling from guaranteed behavior and narrows group ordering to a subscription. Congestion-control prose now refers to signals instead of omniscient capacity measurement.
- Chapter 08 links name ownership enforcement to its normative source.

## Arithmetic and visuals

Independently recalculated: 2 Mb/s × 100 viewers = 200 Mb/s; 4 Mb/s ÷ 8 = 0.5 MB/s; the latency budget totals 200 ms; a 2 Mb/s deficit over five seconds creates a 10 Mb backlog, requiring another five seconds at 2 Mb/s once production stops. The 100 ms timeout diagrams use the correct distinct starting events. The late-join diagram is explicitly a media example, not a requirement that every MOQT group contain a keyframe.

## Limits

The audit deliberately retains draft-21 as the book's baseline; it does not claim that every deployment implements it or that it is a final RFC. No live relay, credential, key distribution, decoder recovery, or network-loss experiment was run. The suggested access-control tests are instructions, not claimed results. The moq.dev pages are moving implementation documentation, checked on the audit date. In particular, their current moq-lite behavior must not be substituted for MOQT draft-21 rules.
