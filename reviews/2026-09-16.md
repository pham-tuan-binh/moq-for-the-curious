# Editorial and technical review — September 16, 2026

Baseline: MOQT draft-ietf-moq-transport-21; QUIC RFCs 9000, 9001, 9002 and 9221; WebRTC RFCs 8835 and 7667; W3C browser APIs; moq.dev implementation documentation.

## Review method

Three reviewers read the complete book between them, with overlapping technical and editorial coverage. Findings were checked against primary sources. The author then revised the manuscript and requested a second review of the revised explanations. This is an editorial review, not protocol interoperability testing or reader usability research.

## First pass

The reviews found no major contradiction in the existing protocol explanations. They did find substantial gaps in teaching depth:

- Chapter 1 introduced transport acronyms before explaining the comparison.
- Chapters 2–3 needed first-use definitions and a scoped object-address example.
- Chapter 4 named session and subscription concepts without showing an actual pinned-draft lifecycle.
- Chapter 6 did not explain delivery timeout anchors or the action taken at expiry.
- Chapter 7 needed a worked playback timeline and a concrete decoder lifecycle.
- Chapters 8–10 needed clearer authentication boundaries, error scopes, and observable demo steps.
- The glossary and references needed links back to explanations and precise source sections.

## Completion criteria

A revised explanation should introduce its terms, connect a concrete problem to a mechanism, and show the resulting behavior. Protocol requirements must distinguish RFCs, the pinned Internet-Draft, and implementation conventions. Illustrative numbers and unexecuted commands must remain labeled. No substantive accuracy or explanation finding should remain unresolved after the follow-up review.

## Revisions and second pass

The manuscript now includes a two-viewer subscription lifecycle, scoped object identity, exact delivery-timer examples, a playback clock table, a decoder API sequence, and an expanded guided upstream demo. First-use definitions and source links were added throughout.

Cross-review found and corrected these smaller issues:

- `PUBLISH_OK` is the draft's name for `REQUEST_OK` answering `PUBLISH`, not a separate wire message.
- Expired datagrams must be dropped; subgroup timeout semantics differ for datagrams.
- Subgroups describe subscription delivery; fetch has a separate representation.
- TLS server authentication does not imply viewer authentication.
- Resource-limit cancellation is each endpoint's responsibility.
- Nix is the selected recommended walkthrough route, not upstream's only supported setup.
- The logging example now matches upstream's documented `RUST_LOG=debug` setting.
- Keyframes, layered video, UDP, TCP, TLS, jitter, and JWT are explained where needed.

The technical follow-up found no remaining material issue in chapters 7–10 or the glossary. The editorial follow-up found the major teaching gaps resolved, subject to the final small definitions and response-alias corrections above. The conceptual book is distinct from an executed implementation tutorial: the upstream demo has not been run as part of this review.

Final verification: the editorial reviewer reread the final corrections and reported no remaining substantive editorial blocker within the reviewed scope. Quarto rendered all 14 pages; the automated checker passed 948 local links and assets. Browser inspection confirmed the new lifecycle, reference, and timing tables fit a 390-pixel mobile viewport without page overflow. The final desktop chapter was also inspected.
